Short, because the audience is three people who live together.

Vesta is a private assistant built for one household. She is not a commercial product, is not offered to the public, and cannot be signed up for. These terms exist because Google asks every application that uses its APIs to publish some, and because it is fair to write down what she does and does not promise.

Who may use her

Only the members of the household that operates her, and the person they have hired to help at home. Access is granted in a private Slack workspace by invitation. There is no public interface.

What she does

Reads and manages the Google Calendars of accounts that have explicitly authorised her, answers questions about them, and keeps written notes she has been asked to keep. What she is permitted to do is described on the home page, and what she does with the data is described in the privacy policy.

No warranty

Vesta is provided as is, without warranty of any kind. She composes answers using a language model and can be wrong: she can misread an event, miscount a free hour, or misunderstand a request. Do not treat her as the authoritative record of anything. The calendar is the record; she is a convenience laid over it.

Anything with a real consequence — money, work hours, medical appointments, commitments made to other people — should be confirmed against the calendar itself before it is relied upon.

Limitation of liability

To the extent permitted by law, the operator of Vesta accepts no liability for any loss arising from her use, including missed appointments, scheduling errors, or actions taken on the basis of an answer she gave.

Ending it

Any connected account may withdraw access at any time from myaccount.google.com/permissions, with no notice and no consequence. The operator may stop running her at any time, for any reason, without warning.
